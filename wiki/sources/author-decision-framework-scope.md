Two author decisions. The methodological framework is Design Science Research, read from "data science research", an interpretation still open to correction. The study is **not** restricted to feature-poor regions: regions vary in object density, and feature-poor areas are only the hardest case.

## Summary
These close the "[Rahmenwerk belegen]" placeholder in the exposé draft. They also widen the problem framing from "feature-poor rural/Alpine regions" to "regions with different object density".

## Key claims
- **Framework: Design Science Research**, artefact plus evaluation. Hevner et al. 2004 is the foundation; Peffers et al. 2007 gives the process model: problem → objectives → design and development → demonstration → evaluation → communication. Both citations come from the assistant's knowledge and are unverified (`expose/kandidaten.bib`) ([[author-decision-framework-scope]]).
- **Scope: not only feature-poor regions.** Object density varies, from villages with many buildings to mountain areas with almost none. Feature-poor areas stay the hardest case and a limit to report ([[author-decision-framework-scope]], [[localization-levers-test]]).

## Relevance to this wiki
It reframes the problem statement in [[expose-material]] and [[thesis-contribution]]. The Miesbach → Garmisch transfer now reads as a transfer between regions of different object density, not as a study of feature-poor areas alone.
