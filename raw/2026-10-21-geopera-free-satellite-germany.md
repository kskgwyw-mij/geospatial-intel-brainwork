## From the article (snippets)
- Author Darcy Weedman; published 28 Oct 2025, updated 11 Jun 2026 (German version: 11 Jun 2026, updated 17 Aug 2026).
- Vendor blog: Geopera's own "Pera Portal" is ranked first.
- Sources in the list include: Pera Portal, Copernicus (Data Space), BKG, GeoPortal.de, Google Earth, state geoportals
  (Landes-Geoportale), ESA Earth Online, NASA Earthdata, Maxar open (disaster) imagery, Zoom Earth.
- Copernicus is called the main free source for European data: Sentinel-2 optical at 10 m (revisit 5 days) and a Sentinel-1 radar archive.
- Free imagery is usually updated only every few months to years and is lower resolution: buildings are visible, detailed features are not.
  High-resolution imagery generally requires payment.

## Related official facts surfaced by the same search (not from the article)
- AdV (surveying authorities of the German Länder), https://www.adv-online.de/Products/Geotopography/Aerial-Photographs/ :
  national digital orthophotos at 20 cm (DOP20) and 40 cm (DOP40), in some Länder also 10 cm; metadata on Geoportal.de.
- North Rhine-Westphalia orthophotos: 10 cm/pixel, renewed in a 3-year cycle
  (https://data.opendatascience.eu/geonetwork/srv/api/records/0ae7c19a-e5b5-4f5e-8556-bca9b3d9e38c).
- Lower Saxony DOP20: actuality about three years. Saxony (Dresden) orthophotos at 20 cm. Mecklenburg-Vorpommern DOP as WMTS
  (https://data.europa.eu/data/datasets/7113c8eb-0bb0-428a-8dca-f607a6124a8f).
- Commercial comparison: Vexcel aerial imagery at 7.5 cm for urban areas (not free), https://vexceldata.com/countries/germany/
